5. Groove & Tempo

Tools for tightening or loosening timing, and for tracking tempo across a song.

Quantize

In the Piano Roll's toolbar (above the note grid), use Quantize to snap notes toward the nearest grid line, by a strength from 0 (no change) to 100% (fully snapped) — it targets your current note selection if you have one, or the whole open Region otherwise. A step-grid lane's steps are already grid-locked by definition, so quantize only applies to the Piano Roll.

Humanize

Humanize nudges timing and velocity by a small pseudo-random jitter, in the Piano Roll toolbar for notes and via a lane's own 🎲 popup menu in the Beats window for steps. There's no per-step selection — a step-grid humanize action always applies to a whole lane at once. The jitter is deterministic given the same seed (based on the click time), which is why re-running it can give a different result each time you click.

Groove templates

A groove template applies a repeating pattern of timing/velocity nudges across a cycle of positions — the same swing feel you'd apply in a drum-machine or DAW groove quantize. Pick one from the Piano Roll toolbar (notes) or a lane's 🎲 menu (steps):

TemplateFeel
StraightNo change — the "off" option.
Swing 8th (Light)Subtle swing on the off-beat 8th notes.
Swing 8th (Heavy)Pronounced swing on the off-beat 8th notes.
Swing 16th (Light)Subtle swing on the 4th 16th-note of each group.
MPC PushPushes alternating positions slightly ahead of the beat, with a velocity boost — a driving, ahead-of-the-beat feel.
MPC Lay-backThe mirror of Push — pulls alternating positions slightly behind the beat with a velocity dip, for a laid-back feel.

A step's timing nudge is capped so it can never cross into a neighboring step's own territory — groove and humanize on a step-grid lane always stay musically unambiguous about which step is which.

Tap tempo

Click the TAP button on the transport LCD in rhythm; after a couple of taps it starts estimating BPM from your average tap interval, averaging up to the last 8 taps. A pause of more than 2 seconds between taps resets the sequence and starts a fresh estimate.

Audio tempo detection

File → Detect Tempo… opens a dialog to analyze a WAV file's tempo from its audio (energy-envelope onset detection, works best on clearly rhythmic material like a drum loop or click track — not a dense full mix) and offers to apply the detected BPM to the song. This is separate from MIDI import's own "apply tempo" option, which just reads a MIDI file's own embedded tempo event rather than analyzing audio.

The tempo map (Smart Tempo)

The song's main BPM field is still the tempo at the very start of the timeline. To change tempo partway through a song, use the Playlist's Tempo Track panel:

  • Insert a point at the playhead — it defaults to whatever tempo is already in effect there, so inserting one alone is a no-op until you adjust its value.
  • Edit a point's BPM in place.
  • Remove a point.

Tempo holds constant from one point to the next (a step function, not a smooth ramp) — there's no dragging a point directly on the Playlist timeline, points are edited by value. Note/step triggering follows tempo changes exactly (each tick re-resolves the tempo in effect); continuous automation and WAV export follow it with buffer-level precision — close enough that a tempo change is inaudible as a seam, but not literally sample-accurate the way trigger timing is.

There's no automatic tempo-curve detection from a recording's actual performance (true beat-tracking across tempo drift) — only the single-BPM audio detector above, and manually placed tempo-map points.
